The government has proposed raising the minimum EPC for rental properties to band C — by 2028 for new tenancies and 2030 for all. The legislation is not yet passed, but the policy direction is firm. London landlords with properties below C should begin planning now to avoid the 2027–2028 installer bottleneck.

## The current state of the legislation

The EPC C minimum for rentals has been through several iterations since it was first proposed in 2021. As of May 2026:

- The **2018–2020 MEES E standard** is law and fully in force
- The **upgrade to C** was formally consulted on in 2023 under the previous government and retained in principle by the current government in 2024
- The **legislation to enact the C standard** has not been introduced to Parliament as of this writing

The proposed dates — **C by 2028 for new tenancies, C by 2030 for all** — have been consistent across the consultation documents. The key uncertainty is timing of legislation, not the direction.

Most property professionals and legal advisers are treating 2028 as a planning deadline, not a hope. Landlords who wait for the law to pass before acting will likely face a surge in installer demand in 2027 and prices to match.

## What "reaching C" means for different London property types

Band C starts at 69 SAP points. The gap depends on your current rating:

| Current Band | SAP Range | Points Needed for C | Typical Route |
|-------------|-----------|--------------------|----|
| D (low) | 55–61 | 8–14 | Insulation + controls |
| D (high) | 62–68 | 1–7 | Often one measure |
| E (low) | 39–50 | 19–30 | Significant works |
| E (high) | 51–54 | 15–18 | Multiple measures |

### Cavity-wall properties (1920s–1990s builds)

Properties in Ealing, Bromley, Enfield, Croydon, Barnet, Sutton, and similar outer-London suburbs built between the 1920s and 1990s are the most straightforward. Cavity wall insulation (£400–£1,500) combined with loft top-up (£400–£700) and LEDs typically delivers the points needed to clear C.

**Estimated total cost to C: £800–£2,200.** Many qualify for ECO4 funding — potentially free.

### Victorian and Edwardian solid-wall properties (pre-1919)

This is the high-risk category. Victorian terraces in Hackney, Islington, Lambeth, Wandsworth, Southwark, Tower Hamlets, and similar inner-London areas typically have solid 9-inch brick walls that cannot be cavity-filled. To reach C from a typical D rating, these properties need:

**Option 1 — External wall insulation (EWI):** Adds rendered insulation board to the outside of the building. Point gain: 10–18 SAP. Cost for a London mid-terrace: £12,000–£18,000 for rear plus front. Planning consent required for front elevations in many areas; conservation area restrictions apply.

**Option 2 — Internal wall insulation (IWI):** Dry-lining on the inside of external walls. Similar point gain to EWI. Cost: £8,000–£14,000 for a 3-bed terrace. Reduces floor area and disrupts decoration and skirtings. Thermal bridging risk at reveals.

**Option 3 — Solar PV as a partial route:** A 3–4kWp system adds 10–20 SAP points. For a Victorian terrace already at D65, solar PV may be sufficient to clear C without wall insulation. Cost: £5,000–£9,000.

**Estimated total cost to C for solid-wall Victorian property: £5,000–£18,000**, depending on current rating and chosen route.

## The £15,000 cost cap and exemptions

Under the proposed C standard, the cost cap is expected to rise to **£15,000 per property** (from the current £3,500 under the E standard). This has major implications for exemptions:

- Under the **current E standard**, many solid-wall properties qualify for a cost-cap exemption because wall insulation alone exceeds £3,500
- Under the **proposed C standard with a £15,000 cap**, fewer properties would qualify — EWI for a London terrace at £12,000–£16,000 straddles the cap boundary
- A property where all relevant improvements genuinely exceed £15,000 (including VAT) would still qualify for an exemption — but the evidence threshold is the same: three installer quotes, all measures under the cap completed first

The exemption structure is expected to mirror the current MEES exemptions: cost cap, consent refusal, devaluation, listed building. All exemptions require registration on the PRS Exemptions Register.

## Why acting before 2027 makes financial sense

### Installer capacity

The current building fabric improvement workforce in the UK is insufficient to upgrade the estimated 1.3 million rental properties below C by 2028. Government analysts have acknowledged this. Demand for EWI contractors, heat pump installers, and loft insulation teams will surge from 2026 onward. Early movers get better availability and negotiating position.

### ECO4 funding window

The ECO4 scheme funds insulation and heating upgrades for eligible households. ECO4 runs until 2026, with a successor scheme expected but not yet confirmed. Landlords with eligible tenants should act now — waiting may mean paying full cost if the scheme changes or ends.

### Between-tenancy timing

Insulation and wall works are significantly less disruptive (and faster) in an empty property. Landlords can plan improvements around void periods rather than working around a tenant in situ. A property improving to C between tenancies can re-let at a higher standard, often at a slight rent premium.

## Practical planning guide for London landlords

**Step 1 — Know your current rating.** Check the government EPC register (find-energy-certificate.service.gov.uk) for each property. If the EPC is more than ten years old, commission a new assessment before making any improvement decisions.

**Step 2 — Identify your property type.** Cavity-wall (post-1920) properties have a much cheaper route to C than solid-wall Victorian terraces. Sort your portfolio by property type.

**Step 3 — Get assessed and scored.** An EPC assessment gives you the exact SAP score, the recommended improvements with estimated costs, and the projected rating after each measure. This is the only reliable planning tool.

**Step 4 — Check ECO4 eligibility.** If any tenant receives Universal Credit, Pension Credit, or other qualifying benefits, the property may qualify for fully funded insulation. Apply before the scheme window closes.

**Step 5 — Schedule works by tenancy.** Plan improvements around void periods where possible. For large works like EWI, you may need to serve notice to access — check your tenancy agreement and give appropriate notice.
