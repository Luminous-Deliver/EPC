An EPC is valid for 10 years from the date of issue. The certificate is tied to the property — not the owner — so a previous owner's certificate is legally usable if it's within the 10-year window. You need a new EPC when the existing one expires, when you've made significant improvements, or when MEES compliance requires a current rating.

## How the 10-year validity period works

The validity period begins on the date the EPC is lodged on the government register, which is normally the same day the assessor submits the data. The exact expiry date is shown on the certificate itself and on the government EPC register (find-energy-certificate.service.gov.uk — search free by postcode).

The certificate is property-specific. It stays on the register regardless of who owns or lets the property. A buyer, new owner, or new landlord can use the existing certificate for selling or letting purposes until it expires — no new assessment is required simply because ownership changed.

## When you must get a new EPC

You are legally required to have a valid EPC in the following circumstances:

### Selling the property

The EPC must be commissioned before marketing begins. If your existing EPC has expired, you cannot legally market the property. You must commission a new assessment before the listing goes live. See our [EPC for selling guide](/blog/epc-for-selling-house) for full detail on timing.

### Renting the property

A valid EPC is required before letting. For MEES purposes (the minimum band E standard), the local authority may require evidence of a valid, in-date EPC. If your certificate expires during a tenancy, you do not need to immediately renew — but you will need a valid EPC before the next letting.

### MEES compliance check

Landlords cannot use an exemption on the PRS Exemptions Register as a substitute for a valid EPC. Some landlords with expired EPCs have found themselves unable to confirm compliance. Commissioners of improvement works also generally require an in-date EPC to register new grant-funded improvements.

## When you might want a new EPC (but aren't legally required)

### After making improvements

If you've added loft insulation, installed cavity wall insulation, replaced the boiler, fitted solar panels, or made any other energy efficiency improvements since your last EPC, a new assessment will typically show a better rating. The improved rating is permanently on the register and is useful for:
- Demonstrating MEES compliance at a higher band
- Supporting a higher rental value
- Strengthening a property listing before sale
- Accessing the Boiler Upgrade Scheme (which requires a current EPC)

There is no waiting period — you can commission a new EPC immediately after works are complete.

### Before the existing EPC expires

If your EPC is approaching expiry and you plan to sell or re-let soon, it's worth commissioning a new assessment in advance rather than letting the certificate lapse and creating urgency. Allow at least a week for scheduling and certificate delivery.

## How to check your EPC expiry date

1. Go to **find-energy-certificate.service.gov.uk** (England and Wales)
2. Search by **postcode** — all EPCs ever issued for properties in that postcode appear
3. Select your property from the list
4. The certificate shows the **date of assessment** and **date of expiry** at the top

This is a free public service. You do not need to create an account.

If no EPC appears for your property, no certificate has been issued (or one was never lodged). For properties built before 2008, this is common — the obligation to have an EPC on sale or letting was phased in between 2007 and 2009.

## EPC validity in common scenarios

| Scenario | Is the existing EPC valid? | Action needed |
|----------|--------------------------|---------------|
| Selling a property you've owned for 8 years, EPC issued 6 years ago | Yes | None — certificate still valid |
| Re-letting after a void period, EPC expired last month | No | Commission new assessment before marketing |
| Buying at auction, previous EPC is 9 years old | Yes (for now) | Plan new assessment within 12 months |
| Just fitted new insulation and want the rating updated | Existing still valid | Optional — commission new assessment to show improved rating |
| HMO licence renewal, EPC expired | No | New assessment required before renewal |
| New-build never previously marketed | None exists | Commission EPC before first sale or let |

## Can I transfer an EPC from one property to another?

No. An EPC is issued for a specific property at a specific address. It cannot be transferred, amended, or reused for a different address. Each property requires its own assessment.

For conversions (e.g., a house converted into flats), each new unit requires its own EPC. The parent property's certificate cannot be used for the individual flats.

## Does the EPC expiry affect a buyer's mortgage?

Some lenders now screen EPC ratings as part of their lending criteria, particularly for buy-to-let mortgages. An expired EPC is generally treated as a missing EPC for mortgage purposes — the lender cannot confirm the current standard. If your buyer's mortgage application requires a current EPC (check with their broker), you may need to commission one even if you're within the 10-year window on a marginal basis.
